Definition of Terms

Clubfoot: A congenital deformity in which the foot is turned inwards and downwards, with a fixed equinus, varus, adduction and cavus position.

Incidence: The number of new clubfoot cases per 1,000 live births at the hospital within the study period.

Ponseti method: A non-surgical treatment using serial casting, Achilles tenotomy and bracing.

Abstract

Clubfoot is a common congenital musculoskeletal deformity that causes lifelong disability if untreated. Most affected children live in low- and middle-income countries, where early diagnosis and treatment are often delayed. Local data on its incidence at Kapkatet Sub-County Hospital are limited. This study will determine the incidence of clubfoot among children aged five years and below at Kapkatet Sub-County Hospital. It will also describe the sociodemographic and clinical characteristics of the affected children. The study will use a retrospective cross-sectional design. The researcher will review records of all children aged five years and below diagnosed with clubfoot between January 2021 and December 2025. Maternity registers will provide the number of live births used as the denominator. A structured data abstraction form on Kobotoolbox will collect the data. The researcher will analyse data using SPSS and present the findings as frequencies, percentages and incidence per 1,000 live births. The study will seek approval from the departmental panel and NACOSTI before data collection. It will protect patient confidentiality by using codes instead of names. The findings will inform early screening, referral and resource planning for clubfoot services in Kericho County.

Key words: clubfoot, congenital talipes equinovarus, incidence, children, Kapkatet

Chapter One: Introduction

Background to the study

Clubfoot, or congenital talipes equinovarus, is a structural deformity of the foot present at birth. The foot points downwards and turns inwards. It affects one or both feet. Globally, the birth prevalence is about one per 1,000 live births, and most cases occur in low- and middle-income countries (Smythe et al., 2017). In sub-Saharan Africa, many children present late because of poor awareness and limited access to services. Untreated clubfoot leads to pain, difficulty walking, stigma and reduced school attendance. The Ponseti method gives good results when treatment starts early. In Kenya, clubfoot clinics have been set up in several hospitals. However, hospital-level data on incidence in Kericho County remain scarce.

Statement of the research problem

Clubfoot is a treatable condition, yet many children in rural Kenya present late or never receive care. Kapkatet Sub-County Hospital serves a large rural catchment area. There is no documented figure on how many children aged five years and below present with clubfoot. Without these data, planners cannot estimate the burden or plan casting, bracing and referral services. This study will address this gap.

Justification

The findings will give the hospital a baseline on clubfoot incidence. They will help managers plan orthopaedic services and train staff on early detection. They will also guide community awareness and referral pathways, and contribute to the limited local literature.

Research questions

1. What is the incidence of clubfoot among children aged five years and below at Kapkatet Sub-County Hospital?
2. What are the sociodemographic characteristics of these children?
3. What are the clinical characteristics of the clubfoot, including laterality and age at presentation?

Objectives

Broad objective: To determine the incidence of clubfoot among children aged five years and below at Kapkatet Sub-County Hospital.

Specific objectives:
1. To determine the incidence of clubfoot per 1,000 live births between January 2021 and December 2025.
2. To describe the sociodemographic characteristics of the affected children.
3. To describe the clinical characteristics and age at presentation of the affected children.

Scope and limitations

The study will cover children aged five years and below treated at one hospital. It will not cover children treated elsewhere. Missing or incomplete records may limit the data. The researcher will exclude incomplete records and report the number excluded.

Conceptual framework

The independent variables will be child factors (sex, age, laterality) and maternal factors (age, residence). The dependent variable will be the occurrence of clubfoot, expressed as incidence per 1,000 live births.

Chapter Three: Materials and Methods

Study setting

The study will be done at Kapkatet Sub-County Hospital in Bureti, Kericho County, Kenya. The hospital offers maternity, paediatric and orthopaedic services to a mainly rural population.

Study design

The study will use a retrospective, hospital-based cross-sectional design. This design suits the objective of estimating incidence from existing records.

Study population

The target population will be all children aged five years and below diagnosed with clubfoot at the hospital between January 2021 and December 2025.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Inclusion: Children aged five years and below with a clinical diagnosis of idiopathic clubfoot recorded in the hospital files or clinic register.

Exclusion: Records with missing key data, and children with syndromic or neurological clubfoot such as spina bifida or arthrogryposis.

Variables

Dependent: Occurrence of clubfoot (incidence per 1,000 live births).
Independent: Sex, age at presentation, laterality, maternal age, place of residence.

Sampling techniques and sample size

The researcher will use a census of all eligible records. No sample size calculation will be needed. The denominator will be the total live births in the hospital maternity register for the same period.

Data collection tool and process

The researcher will design a structured data abstraction form and load it on Kobotoolbox. The researcher will collect data from clinic registers, patient files and the maternity register after approval.

Pre-testing

The researcher will pre-test the form on 10 records. These records will not be included in the final analysis. The researcher will adjust unclear items.

Validity and reliability

Supervisors will review the form for content validity. The researcher will check 10% of the entered records against the source to confirm accuracy and consistency.

Data analysis

The researcher will clean and analyse data using SPSS. Incidence will be calculated as the number of new clubfoot cases divided by live births, multiplied by 1,000. Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and percentages. The researcher will present results in tables and figures.

Ethical considerations

The researcher will complete research ethics training and obtain a certificate. The proposal will be defended before the departmental panel. The department will seek a NACOSTI licence before data collection. The researcher will obtain permission from the hospital administration. Patient names will not be recorded. Codes will replace identifiers. Data will be stored in a password-protected device and used only for this study, in line with the Data Protection Act, 2019.
